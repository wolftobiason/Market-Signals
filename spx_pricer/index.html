Methodology

Model: Black-Scholes (1973)

Uses the Black-Scholes-Merton framework — the standard closed-form model for European-style options. SPY options are technically American-style, but early exercise is rarely optimal (except for deep ITM puts near ex-dividend dates), making Black-Scholes a reliable approximation in most cases.

Pricing Formulas

d₁ = [ ln(S/K) + (r + σ²/2)·T ] / ( σ·√T )
d₂ = d₁ − σ·√T

Call = S·N(d₁) − K·e−rT·N(d₂)
Put = K·e−rT·N(−d₂) − S·N(−d₁)

N(·) = standard normal CDF  ·  S = spot  ·  K = strike  ·  r = risk-free rate  ·  σ = vol  ·  T = years to expiry

Greeks

Δ Delta = ∂V/∂S → sensitivity to $1 move in spot
Γ Gamma = ∂²V/∂S² → rate of change of delta
Θ Theta = ∂V/∂t → daily time decay
ν Vega = ∂V/∂σ → P&L per 1% increase in vol
ρ Rho = ∂V/∂r → P&L per 1% increase in rate

Implied Volatility

When a market price is provided, IV is solved via bisection — iteratively narrowing a bracket until the model price matches the market price to within $0.001.

Live Data

Spot, VIX, and 3M T-bill rate are fetched from Yahoo Finance on load and refresh every 30 seconds. Spot pre-fills automatically. VIX seeds the vol input as an ATM 30-day approximation — adjust for your specific strike/tenor. Rate uses the 13-week T-bill yield (^IRX).

Assumptions & Limitations

Continuous log-normal returns — no jumps
Constant vol over option life (flat vol surface)
Constant risk-free rate
No transaction costs or taxes
SPY dividend yield not modeled — use Merton extension for precision
Real markets exhibit volatility skew not captured here
