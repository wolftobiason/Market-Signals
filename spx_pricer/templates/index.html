Methodology

Model: Black-Scholes (1973)

This pricer uses the Black-Scholes-Merton framework, the standard closed-form model for European-style options. SPX options are European — they can only be exercised at expiration — making Black-Scholes the appropriate baseline model.

Pricing Formulas

d₁ = [ ln(S/K) + (r + σ²/2)·T ] / ( σ·√T )
d₂ = d₁ − σ·√T

Call = S·N(d₁) − K·e−r·T·N(d₂)
Put = K·e−r·T·N(−d₂) − S·N(−d₁)

Where N(·) is the standard normal CDF, S = spot price, K = strike, r = risk-free rate, σ = volatility, T = time to expiry in years.

Greeks

Δ (Delta) = ∂V/∂S → option price sensitivity to $1 move in spot
Γ (Gamma) = ∂²V/∂S² → rate of change of delta per $1 move
Θ (Theta) = ∂V/∂t → daily time decay (shown per calendar day)
ν (Vega) = ∂V/∂σ → P&L per 1% increase in implied vol
ρ (Rho) = ∂V/∂r → P&L per 1% increase in risk-free rate

Implied Volatility

Black-Scholes has no closed-form inverse for σ. When a market price is provided, this pricer uses Brent's method — a root-finding algorithm that brackets the solution and converges to the IV that equates the model price to the observed market price. Convergence is typically achieved in under 50 iterations.

Model Assumptions & Limitations

Continuous log-normal returns (no jumps)
Constant volatility over option life
Constant risk-free rate
No transaction costs or taxes
SPX pays a continuous dividend yield (not modeled here — use Black-76 or Merton extension for accuracy)
Real markets exhibit volatility skew — Black-Scholes uses a flat vol surface
Fat tails and vol clustering are not captured
European exercise only — correct for SPX (not SPY)
